The Evolution of Omak: 1888 to Today

"From the silver picks of the Okanogan Highlands to the buzzing timber saws of Biles-Coleman, to the golden rows of apple orchards—and now, the welcoming frontier of outdoor recreation, cultural heritage, and world-class tourism."

1888–1910

1. The Mining Frontier

Before Omak was incorporated, silver and lead rushes boomed in nearby Ruby City and Conconully. Prospectors and traders forged the very trails that became the wagon roads and river crossings of Central Washington.

1921–1970

2. Timber & The Biles-Coleman Mill

J.C. Biles and Nate Coleman founded the monumental Biles-Coleman Lumber Company in Omak in 1921. It was one of the largest pine remanufacturing plants in the world, bringing the railroad, thousands of family jobs, and civic growth.

1930–2010

3. World Apple & Orchard Capital

With federal irrigation projects feeding the Okanogan basin, thousands of fertile acres transformed into world-renowned Red Delicious, Golden Delicious, and cherry orchards. Packing houses and cold storage facilities lined the riverbanks.

Today & Beyond

4. Cultural & Outdoor Tourism Era

As global agricultural markets shifted, Omak embraced its true sovereign calling: a premier Pacific Northwest destination. With the legendary Omak Stampede, the Colville Indian Encampment, regional soccer/baseball invitationals, the Okanogan River, and Hotel-Motel Tax reinvestment, Omak has become the cultural heart of the valley.
