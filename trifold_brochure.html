THE STORY OF OMAK

"From pioneer mining trails to timber and orchards—now a thriving Pacific Northwest tourism frontier."

1888–1910: Mining Frontier

Silver rushes in nearby Ruby City and Conconully forged early wagon roads across the Okanogan River.

1921–1970: Timber & The Big Mill

J.C. Biles and Nate Coleman founded Biles-Coleman Lumber Co., making Omak a global timber and rail center.

1930–2010: Apple Capital

Federal irrigation transformed the valley into world-famed Red Delicious and cherry orchards.

Today: The Tourism Frontier

Omak now reinvests Lodging Tax funds directly into sports tourism, the historic Stampede, and river recreation.

Funded by Municipal Lodging Tax (RCW 67.28): Reinvesting Hotel-Motel revenues directly into Eastside Park recreation infrastructure.

FIELD & FACILITY DIRECTORY

1. Joe Robbins Field Little League

Youth lighted ballpark with dual-lane batting cages. Honoring mid-century pioneer Joe Robbins who chartered Omak’s first Little League.

2. John Pock Field Youth Minors

Developmental diamond across from the pool. Dedicated to booster John Pock who ensured every child had cleats and a glove.

3. Ross McCormick Field High School

Varsity baseball diamond with press box and bleachers. Commemorating legendary coach Ross McCormick.

4. Jack Rowe Field Tournament

Multi-use diamond modernized with an all-weather infield for year-round state and regional tournament play.

5 & 6. Bowden & Buchanon Fastpitch

Dedicated high school fastpitch softball diamonds honoring Shirley Bowden and Stephanie Buchanon.

North Omak Soccer Complex Fields 1–4

Four pristine grass pitches hosting youth leagues, high school matches, and "Kick'N It with the Cops".

Carl Precht Memorial RV Park 68 Sites

Shaded by mature Catalpa trees along the river. Honoring pioneer elementary educator and outdoor leader Carl Precht.
